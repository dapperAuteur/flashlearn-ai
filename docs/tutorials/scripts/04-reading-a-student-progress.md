# Video script 04: Reading a student's progress

{{video:PENDING}}

**What the viewer will be able to do:** open one student's record from the classroom roster, tell
which numbers to act on before the next lesson, and read an empty record as an absence rather than a
bad score.

**Length:** aim for 5 to 6 minutes.

---

## Setup before you record

| Needs to be true | Why |
|---|---|
| Signed in as a Teacher, Tutor, Parent, SchoolAdmin, or Admin | A Student account is redirected out of the teacher area, and cannot read anyone's progress |
| You teach an **active** classroom, or the student is linked to your account | These are the only two ways in. The Admin role on its own opens nothing |
| One student on the roster who **has** finished several sessions, across **two or more sets** | **By set** is a one-row table otherwise, which makes it look pointless |
| That student has **missed at least three cards**, ideally in different sets | **Cards to go over** is the payoff. An empty one turns beat 6 into an apology |
| A second student on the roster who has **never** finished a session | Beat 4 needs the real empty state. It cannot be faked once numbers exist |
| At least one session on the first student was run **by you**, and one **by the student alone** | So the **Studied** column shows both **With an adult** and **On their own** |
| Some of that studying happened in the last 30 days | Otherwise **Accuracy, last 30 days** reads **No answers in this window**, which needs explaining mid-take |
| **Online** | The page is a live read. There is no offline copy of it |
| Test students, or a blur tool ready | Real names and real card fronts are on screen for most of the video |

---

## The beats

### 1. Open with what this page is not

**On screen:** just you, or a title card. Do not open the app yet.

**Say:** "This is not a report card and it is not a grade. It is the answer to one question: what do
I ask this student about in the next ten minutes. Everything on the page is ordered worst first for
exactly that reason. And one thing before we start, because it is the mistake that costs people the
most: a blank record and a bad record look nothing alike here, and we are going to spend a beat on
why."

**Why it matters:** sets the reading frame. A viewer who arrives expecting a grade will treat the
accuracy number as a verdict and ignore the list that is actually useful.

### 2. Getting there

**On screen:** go to **Classrooms**, open your classroom, scroll to the roster. Point at one row.
Every row has **Start session** and **Progress**. Click **Progress**.

**Say:** "Two buttons on every student. Start session runs a session with them. Progress reads what
has already happened. Same roster, same student, opposite directions."

**Why it matters:** the two buttons sit next to each other and do very different things. Naming the
difference once prevents a misclick that writes a real session.

### 3. The header, and the one line that only appears sometimes

**On screen:** the page loads. Point at the heading, which reads the student's name followed by
**progress**. Point at **Back to the roster** above it. If this student is a class account, read the
line under the heading: **This is a class account you created. It has no email address, so
everything here was recorded in a session you ran together.**

**Say:** "Their name, and the way back. If it is an account you created for them, you get that line,
and it is worth taking literally: nothing on this page happened without you in the room, because
that account cannot sign in anywhere else."

**Why it matters:** stops a teacher reading a class account's low numbers as a student who is not
practising at home. That student has no way to practise at home.

### 4. Now the student who has never studied

**On screen:** go back to the roster and open **Progress** on the second student, the one with no
sessions. The whole page is one block headed **No study sessions yet**, with the line: **has not
finished a study session, so there is nothing to score. This is not the same as a low score: there is
no accuracy, no card record, and no time to report yet.** Point at it and pause.

**Say:** "Read that sentence twice. It does not say zero. Zero would mean they answered questions and
got them all wrong, which is a real and quite serious thing to know about a student. This says
nothing has been recorded, which tells you nothing about the student whatsoever. If you go into a
parent conversation with those two mixed up, you will say something you cannot take back. So the page
refuses to print a zero it does not have, here and everywhere else: you will see No answers yet, Not
recorded, None yet, Not scored yet, and every one of those is the page telling you it has nothing
rather than guessing."

**Why it matters:** this is the beat the whole video exists to deliver. Say it slowly and do not cut
it for length.

### 5. Back to the student with a record: the short version

**On screen:** open the first student's **Progress**. Stay on the block headed **The short version**.
Point at each tile as you name it: **Accuracy, all time**, **Accuracy, last 30 days**, **Cards
right**, **Cards wrong**, **Sessions finished**, **Time studied**. Then point at the two lines
underneath, ending at **Average session score**.

**Say:** "Six numbers. Read the two accuracies as a pair, not one at a time. All time is their whole
history, the last 30 days is who they are now, and the gap between them is the only thing on this
block that tells you a direction. Cards right and cards wrong are what the accuracy above is
calculated from, deliberately, so the numbers agree with each other instead of being three separate
measurements you have to reconcile. And down here, average session score is the figure the student
sees on their own dashboard, averaged a different way. If it sits a couple of points off the accuracy
above, both are right, they are answering different questions, and the page says so rather than
hiding one of them."

**Why it matters:** two accuracy figures that do not match generate a support ticket unless somebody
explains, once, that the difference is on purpose.

### 6. Cards to go over, which is the reason to open the page at all

**On screen:** scroll to **Cards to go over**, with its count in brackets and the line **Every card
this student has answered wrong at least once, worst first.** Read across one row: **Card**, **Set**,
**Right**, **Wrong**, **Accuracy**, **Hardest mode**.

**Say:** "This is the part I would actually use. Every card they have missed even once, worst first.
Note that: once, not twice. The review scheduler waits longer than that before it changes how often a
card comes back, and it is right to, because one miss can be a slip. This list does not wait, because
you are choosing what to ask about in the next ten minutes, not rescheduling anything. And the last
column earns its place: hardest mode tells you which direction the card falls over in. A student who
knows a fact front to back but not back to front does not have a gap in the fact, they have a gap in
the recall, and those want different practice."

**Why it matters:** it is the only block on the page that turns straight into a lesson plan, and the
one-miss rule is surprising enough to look like a bug if nobody says it out loud.

### 7. By set

**On screen:** scroll to **By set**, headed with a count and the line **Weakest set first.** Point at
the **Cards seen** column specifically.

**Say:** "Same idea, one level up. Weakest set first, so the top row is where the time should go.
Cards seen is the one people skip: it is how many cards in that set the student has any record for,
so a low accuracy on four cards seen out of forty is not a student who is struggling with the set,
it is a student who has barely met it."

**Why it matters:** accuracy on a tiny sample is the easiest wrong conclusion on the page, and
**Cards seen** is the column that prevents it.

### 8. Recent sessions, and who was in the room

**On screen:** scroll to **Recent sessions**. Read across: **Date**, **Set**, **Right**, **Wrong**,
**Accuracy**, **Time**, **Studied**. Point at a row reading **With an adult** and a row reading **On
their own**.

**Say:** "The last ten finished sessions. Finished is doing work in that sentence: a session someone
walked away from halfway is not here and is not in any number above. And the last column is the one I
would not skip. With an adult, or on their own. When a score looks surprising in either direction,
that column is usually the explanation, because a session you ran together is a different kind of
evidence than one they did alone at home."

**Why it matters:** teaches the viewer to check the conditions before drawing a conclusion from a
number.

### 9. Where the numbers come from, and who else can see them

**On screen:** scroll to the last block, **Where these numbers come from**, and let the paragraph sit
on screen long enough to read.

**Say:** "Two things this block is telling you. First, these are the same records the student reads
on their own progress page, so you and they will never be looking at different figures for the same
thing, which means you can talk about a number on this page with them directly. Second, a session an
adult ran counts toward the student and never toward the adult. If you have run ten sessions for this
student, that work is on their page, not yours. As for who else can open this: whoever can run a
study session for a student can read their progress, and nobody else. That is a classroom you teach
that is still active, or a student linked to your account. Not the Teacher role by itself, not the
Admin role by itself. If you ask for someone you are not connected to, the page tells you so and
shows you nothing."

**Why it matters:** answers the privacy question and the attribution question in one pass, before a
parent or a colleague asks either.

### 10. Close on the button you are most likely to want next

**On screen:** point at **Start a session** at the foot of the page. Do not click it.

**Say:** "And because you almost always come here on your way to doing something about it, the same
Start a session button is at the bottom, already pointed at this student. I am not clicking it,
because it would put a real session on a real account, and that is the last thing I will say about
this page: everything on it is real."

**Why it matters:** ends on the action the page is designed to lead to, while modelling the caution
the button deserves.

## Careful while recording

- **Both Start a session buttons are live**, the one in the empty state and the one at the foot of
  the page, and both open study already pointed at that student. Anything you answer lands on their
  real account and their real review schedule. Point at them, do not click them.
- **The empty state is a one-take opportunity.** The moment you finish a session for that student,
  beat 4 is gone and you cannot get it back without a fresh account.
- **The Admin role alone opens nothing.** Access comes from an active classroom you teach or a
  student linked to your account, so an admin recording this on a student they have no connection to
  will get the refusal message rather than a page, and will reasonably think the feature is broken.
- **An archived classroom does not count.** If the classroom was archived, every student on it
  refuses, and the roster gives you no hint that this is why.
- **Some blocks are capped and do not say so.** Recent sessions shows the last ten and Cards to go
  over shows the worst twenty. A heavy student will have more than you can see, so do not tell the
  viewer it is everything.
- **A quiet month reads as empty.** If the student has not studied recently, **Accuracy, last 30
  days** reads **No answers in this window** while the all-time figure is fine. That looks broken on
  camera and is not.
- **A student inside the account deletion grace period refuses with a different message.** Check the
  roster does not show **This account is scheduled for deletion** on your chosen student first.
- Real student names and real card fronts are on screen for most of this. Use test accounts, or blur.
